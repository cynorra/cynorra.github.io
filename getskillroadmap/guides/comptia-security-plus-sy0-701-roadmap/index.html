CompTIA revised the Security+ blueprint to reflect cloud convergence and Zero Trust architectures:

[Zero Trust Enterprise Perimeter]
             │

  • 1.General Security Concepts (12%): Fundamental cryptographic primitives, authentication controls (MFA, AAA, RADIUS, TACACS+), and Zero Trust principles.
  • 2.Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations (22%): Social engineering, malware taxonomy, application vulnerability discovery, and CVE analysis.
  • 3.Security Architecture (18%): Network segmentation, secure cloud models, micro-segmentation, and high-availability design.
  • 4.Security Operations (28%): The heaviest domain. Covers incident response triage, logging protocols (Syslog, SIEM, SOAR), firewall ACL configurations, and forensic preservation.
  • 5.Security Program Management and Oversight (20%): Risk frameworks (NIST RMF, CSF, ISO 27001), vendor management, and compliance governance.

2. Conquering Performance-Based Questions (PBQs)

The first 3 to 5 questions on the exam are interactive simulations (PBQs). Failing to manage time here causes most candidates to fail:

  • •The PBQ Skip Strategy: Immediately flag all PBQs upon opening the exam. Advance directly to the standard multiple-choice questions. Answering 80+ standard questions establishes your timing cadence and builds cognitive confidence.
  • •Firewall ACL Simulation Matrix: Memorize standard port assignments and default-deny rule order:

3. CEU Renewal Economics: The 3-Year Maintenance Cost

Security+ credentials expire after three years. You have three paths to maintain active status:

  • 1.Earn 50 Continuing Education Units (CEUs): Submit relevant technical webinars, conferences, and coursework plus pay the annual $50 CompTIA maintenance fee ($150 total over three years).
  • 2.Pass CompTIA CertMaster CE: Complete an automated self-paced recertification module for approximately $199.
  • 3.Earn a Higher-Tier Credential: Passing CompTIA CySA+ or PenTest+ automatically resets and renews your Security+ cycle. Compare our Google Cybersecurity vs. CompTIA Security+ Audit for entry alternatives.

SOC Analyst Portfolio: Wazuh SIEM Deployment & Incident Response Playbook

Do not present an empty resume with just a certification badge. Build a verifiable defensive engineering lab:

  • •Target Endpoint: Windows 10/11 VM configured with Microsoft Sysmon (System Monitor) logging detailed process creation (Event ID 1) and network connections (Event ID 3).
  • •Attacker Machine: Kali Linux executing basic Nmap scans and brute-force SSH simulations.
  • •SIEM Centralization: A Linux server running Splunk Free or the Elastic Stack (ELK) ingesting Sysmon logs via the Splunk Universal Forwarder.
  • •Documentation: Write a detailed incident response report documenting detection rules and commit the configuration files to GitHub.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I pass Security+ without Network+ or A+?

Yes. While CompTIA recommends Network+, thousands of candidates skip directly to Security+. If you understand basic TCP/IP networking, IP addressing, and common ports, you can successfully self-study for SY0-701.

Q: How do I qualify for the CompTIA academic student discount?

Enrolled university students can purchase vouchers through the CompTIA Academic Store using their verified .edu credentials, reducing the exam price from $404 to approximately $260.

Q: How do recruiters verify CompTIA certifications?

CompTIA partners with Credly. Provide your permanent Credly verification link on your resume and LinkedIn profile to allow instant verification (see our guide on How to Spot Unaccredited Certificates & Verification Checks).
